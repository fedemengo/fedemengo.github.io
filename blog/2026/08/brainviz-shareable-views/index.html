Brainviz: a portable, extensible 3D brain viewer

August 3, 2026

2026 · projects · brainviz neuroscience visualization

Brainviz is a portable, extensible 3D viewer for human brain structure in MNI space. It brings cortical parcellations, subcortical structures, connectomes, and white-matter tract bundles into one scene—so relationships that are usually split across figures can be explored together.

One network, many layers

Start with the default mode network (DMN). The same focus is resolved on the cortex, structural connectome, and white-matter tracts: one selection, several complementary views of its anatomy.

Default mode network, resolved simultaneously on the cortex, structural connectome, and white-matter tracts.

Combine networks and functional sets

Selections stack. This scene combines two Yeo-7 networks—the default mode and frontoparietal control networks—with language and multiple-demand sets. Their colours, strengths, visible layers, and camera are all part of the saved scene.

Default mode and frontoparietal control networks, layered with language and multiple-demand functional sets.

Open in Brainviz ↗

See the geometry move

Some spatial relationships make more sense in motion. This rotation shows the default mode and frontoparietal control networks on the inflated cortex, together with the connectome and tract layers.

Export a figure

The viewer can also become a presentation-ready image. Brainviz exports a transparent, tightly cropped PNG; the legend can be included so a figure remains interpretable outside the viewer. Here, faded left-hemisphere auditory association and early auditory regions reveal the tractography passing through and beyond them.
